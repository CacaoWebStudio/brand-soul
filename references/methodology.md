## State detection

Assess three independent fields: maturity (`new`, `emerging`, `existing`), evidence coverage (`sparse`, `partial`, `substantial`), and identity coherence (`undefined`, `inconsistent`, `coherent`). Reassess after material evidence changes.

For existing brands, excavate before inventing. For emerging brands, locate real signals before proposing coherence. For new brands, facilitate conscious choices rather than generating a mission, values, tone, and audience from a short prompt.

Repository state is not brand state. An empty workspace, a new repository, or no local evidence must never cause an existing brand to be classified as new. Determine maturity from the user's explicit selection and brand evidence.

## Conversational discovery

Use research-first, bounded elicitation for every user-facing Build workflow:

1. Present operating modes through the host's interactive choice tool when available. Include separate choices for an existing brand and a new brand idea.
2. Ask one question per turn and wait for the answer, but do not equate sequential pacing with an unlimited interview.
3. For an existing brand, ask for its primary website first. Inspect it deeply enough to discover official social profiles and other public sources through navigation, footer links, contact and about pages, structured data, sitemaps, outbound links, and public search.
4. Present the discovered source inventory and ask the founder to validate it. Use tappable **Correct**, **Add or correct sources**, and **Use only selected sources** choices when available. Do not ask the founder to manually list sources already linked or readily discoverable.
5. Inspect public sources before conducting the founder interview. Offer private materials as optional enrichment, not a gate.
6. Convert the evidence brief into a maximum of five initial identity-critical questions. Ask fewer when the sources already answer them.
7. Prefer validation prompts: “I found X and interpret it as Y; is that correct?” Avoid open recall questions when a proposed finding can be confirmed or corrected.
8. A clarification replaces one of the remaining questions. Do not let every answer generate a new branch of interrogation.
9. Keep conversational transitions compact: briefly record the answer, then ask the next question. Do not reproduce a full audit memorandum after every response.
10. After the question budget, produce the draft with explicit gaps. Let the user decide whether another interview pass or a separate claim audit is worth doing.

If the host lacks an interactive choice tool, use a short numbered fallback. Never imitate a control with a long Markdown questionnaire.

## Evidence hierarchy

Weight each source for the specific claim using proximity, recency, independence, incentives, and corroboration. Default authority order:

1. Direct primary records and independently verifiable current evidence
2. Product, operational, legal, or certification records
3. Contemporaneous internal records
4. Founder firsthand memory, labeled as memory
5. Current official company material
6. Employee or partner firsthand accounts
7. Repeated customer behavior or language
8. Historical official marketing
9. Third-party editorial material
10. Social posts, isolated reviews, and unsourced summaries
11. AI-generated or derivative material

Founder authority decides Identity; it does not verify objective history merely by assertion.

## Discovery

Inventory sources and register evidence before interpreting it. Extract atomic candidates without writing new brand copy. Look for facts, stories, recurring language, decisions, product reality, relationships, customer language, contradictions, lost identity, brand debt, possible tensions, and boundaries.

Create an evidence brief containing:

- Supported realities
- Historical chronology
- Repeated signals and their source independence
- Candidate interpretations, labeled as inference
- Conflicts stated neutrally
- Critical gaps
- Evidence-driven interview agenda

Stop source review when additional material is repetitive and remaining critical gaps require a founder decision or firsthand clarification.

## Existing-brand excavation

Build a chronology, identify constants and strategic eras, then classify historical elements as restore, retain historically, supersede, or unresolved. Restore only when an element remains true or deliberately desired, fits present reality, is distinctive, and receives founder approval. Age alone is not authority.

## Founder interview

Before asking anything, rank gaps by whether the answer will materially change protected Identity. Use this order:

1. Identity-critical gaps about purpose, audience, origin, principles, boundaries, voice, and consequential choices
2. Material contradictions that alter Identity or the safe wording of a central claim
3. Operational substantiation such as certificates, invoices, pricing records, authorization documents, legal structures, and impact measurement

Ask from the first two groups within a five-question initial budget. Put the third group in the claim and issue registers unless the user explicitly requests a claim audit. Brand Soul may legitimately conclude that a meaningful part of the brand is aspirational, weakly evidenced, disputed, or not yet ready for unrestricted public use. Map that weakness; do not interrogate it out of existence.

Ask one question per turn. Prefer confirming or correcting a proposed interpretation derived from evidence. Internally classify the answer as fact, memory, interpretation, Identity decision, or Strategy decision without making the founder navigate those categories.

Treat a sufficient answer about the brand's Identity, belief, intention, or lived practice as closure for the Build pass. Do not recursively decompose it into progressively narrower questions about proof or scope. When a statement remains founder-asserted rather than independently verified, record that status, assign a downstream claim permission where relevant, and continue.

Prefer concrete prompts about episodes, costs, rejected alternatives, skeptical counterexamples, and hard choices when they are needed to distinguish Identity. Test claimed principles only when the result would predict a consequential choice.

After each answer, respond compactly. Accumulate records internally and present them together in the draft instead of returning an audit memorandum after every turn. A clarification uses the remaining budget; it does not extend it.

End the initial interview after five questions at most, or earlier when protected Identity is decision-ready. Produce a `draft` or `usable_with_gaps` repository and surface unresolved issues. Ask whether the founder wants to approve, correct the draft, conduct another focused pass, or run a separate claim audit.

When founder intention conflicts with behavior or evidence, state the conflict neutrally. Ask about it only if resolving it changes Identity materially; otherwise register it as unresolved rather than extending the interview. Never polish the conflict away.

## New-brand creation

Establish business reality, motivations, constraints, product, market, and resources. Generate two or three materially different directions, including benefits, costs, risks, tensions, and likely boundaries. Require the founder to select, modify, or reject them. Preserve rejected alternatives only when they clarify a consequential boundary.

Convert chosen directions into proposals and test them against plausible hard decisions before approval. The Skill proposes; the founder decides.

## Tensions

Formalize a tension only when both poles are active and predict a consequential choice. Record its poles, resolution, origin, behavioral implications, failure at each extreme, evidence IDs, and approval. Reject decorative oppositions and universally desirable balances.

## Boundaries

Define prohibited claims, embellished stories, language, customer relationships, growth tactics, assumed cultural authority, aesthetic directions, and rejected opportunities. Record rationale, applicability, examples, exceptions, and approval. Avoid absolutes unless the founder knowingly accepts the commitment.

## Voice

Model relationship, sentence behavior, rhythm, formality, emotional register, specificity, rhetorical patterns, preferred and prohibited vocabulary, and clichés. Label observed examples with evidence IDs. Label generated examples as synthetic and require founder approval before presenting them as canonical examples. Avoid personality sliders.

## Distinctiveness

Remove the brand and product names, compare the text with plausible competitors, and highlight transferable claims. Score specific provenance, consequential choice, falsifiability, and competitor exclusivity from 0–2 each. A central protected section needs at least 6/8 and no central proposition that transfers unchanged to a plausible competitor.
